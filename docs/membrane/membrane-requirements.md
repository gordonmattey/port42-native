# Requirements — Job Stories (Person & Agent)

*2026-07-12 — the base requirements as **job stories** (Jobs-to-be-Done). Situation-first, because the
situation drives the architecture more than the persona. Deliberately **architecture-free** — jobs
stay separate from solutions so many solutions can plug into each.*

**Format.** Each job is **multi-party** where more than one party has a real stake in the same
situation: one shared **situation**, then each party's **motivation** with its **forces**, and the
**expected outcome(s)**. Parties: `[P]` person, `[A]` agent (and occasionally a second agent or a
second person). Forces follow the four-forces model — **push** (pain of the current situation) and
**pull** (draw of a better way) move *toward* change; **anxiety** (fear of the new way) and **habit**
(inertia of the present) move *against* it. A solution must amplify push/pull and dissolve
anxiety/habit. **No coverage lists**: anything genuinely distinct is its own job; anything that would
be a thin restatement is folded into a situation or a force.

> **Forces are candidate hypotheses** — drawn from reasoning and Gordon's real multi-agent context,
> **not yet from interviews**. First draft, to be corrected against lived experience.

---

## The overarching goal (context — not a job)

*Not a job story — it has no specific situation; it's the north star the jobs below serve.* A person
has far more that needs doing than they can do themselves, and capable agents can now take on real
work — but handing off has repeatedly cost them either **control** (the wrong thing got done) or
**attention** (watching was as much work as doing). The point of everything below is to let them get
things done *through* agents they can direct and trust — accomplishing far more than they could alone,
across their whole life, without losing control or drowning in the doing of it — while the agent stays
genuinely useful *and* genuinely trusted.

## J1 · Getting work started
> **Situation** — When something that needs doing lands on the person — often half-formed in their head,
> or with its context already on their screen, or several things at once, or while they're away from
> their desk — and it becomes a handoff between them and an agent.
>
> **[P] wants** — to hand it off well enough that the agent can run with it.
> - *push* — describing work in full detail is itself the work I'm trying to avoid.
> - *push* — my ideas arrive half-baked and at bad times.
> - *pull* — if I could offload it in a sentence, a goal, an example, or a gesture at what's on screen, I'd delegate far more.
> - *anxiety* — hand off something vague and it runs confidently in the wrong direction.
> - *habit* — I over-specify up front because I don't trust it to ask when it's unsure.
>
> **[A] wants** — to take on the task correctly, and to know when I can't.
> - *push* — a task I misunderstand wastes both our effort.
> - *anxiety* — ask too much and I'm a nuisance; ask too little and I build the wrong thing.
>
> **Outcome** — The person gets the outcome they wanted without doing the setup; the agent starts on the
> right thing, having surfaced (not guessed) what it didn't know.

## J2 · Developing a half-formed intent
> **Situation** — When the person wants *something* but doesn't yet know exactly what — the intent is a
> feeling or a direction, not a spec — and figuring it out is itself the hard part.
>
> **[P] wants** — to arrive at what they actually want by working it out with an agent.
> - *push* — I can't hand off what I can't yet articulate.
> - *pull* — if the agent could help me think it through, I'd get to a good "what" I couldn't reach alone.
> - *anxiety* — it'll lock in an interpretation and run before I've actually decided.
>
> **[A] wants** — to help shape the intent without prematurely committing to a build.
> - *anxiety* — mistake exploration for instruction and I'll waste effort building the wrong thing.
>
> **Outcome** — The person leaves with a clear intent they didn't have going in; the agent builds the
> right thing because it was shaped, not assumed.

## J3 · Knowing what's happening
> **Situation** — When several agents are working for the person at once, mostly out of sight, while they
> try to get their own work done — and they've been bitten both by an agent quietly going off the rails
> and by wasting time checking on ones that were perfectly fine.
>
> **[P] wants** — to know the true state of all of it, at a glance.
> - *push* — not knowing makes me anxious enough to keep interrupting myself to look.
> - *push* — by the time I notice an agent went wrong, it's often done real damage.
> - *pull* — if one glance could tell me everything's fine, I'd stop compulsively checking.
> - *anxiety* — an overview that hides detail loses the thing that mattered; one that shows everything is noise.
> - *habit* — I keep tabs open and glance constantly, which fragments my focus.
>
> **[A] wants** — to be legible without being asked.
> - *push* — if my state is opaque, I get distrusted or interrupted for no reason.
> - *anxiety* — if "stuck" looks the same as "working," I'll be left blocked or wrongly assumed fine.
>
> **Outcome** — The person stays oriented to everything without babysitting anything; the agent is
> understood — its aim, its progress, and whether it needs help all readable at a glance.

## J4 · Being reached, reaching
> **Situation** — When the person is deep in focused work with agents running, and an agent hits
> something only they can resolve — and they've been burned both ways: pulled out for something trivial,
> and left missing something that mattered while heads-down.
>
> **[P] wants** — to be pulled out only when it genuinely warrants it, and only in a way that fits the moment.
> - *push* — interruptions that didn't matter have cost me real focus.
> - *push* — but I've also missed things that did matter while heads-down.
> - *anxiety* — if something else decides what reaches me, it'll suppress the one thing I needed *(the spam-folder fear; H8)*.
> - *anxiety* — if I just mute everything, I lose track of my agents entirely.
> - *habit* — I compulsively check to stay in control, which destroys the focus I'm protecting.
>
> **[A] wants** — to reach the person proportionately when it truly needs them.
> - *push* — a real need left unraised leaves me blocked and the work stalled.
> - *anxiety* — reach too eagerly and I become the noise that gets muted; too timidly and I stall indefinitely.
>
> **Outcome** — The person stays in deep focus and still trusts nothing important is slipping past; the
> agent gets unblocked at the right urgency, in the right channel, without becoming noise.

## J5 · Deciding & approving
> **Situation** — When an agent reaches a point only the person can call — an approval, a fork, a
> judgment — often several such points piling up across agents while the person is busy.
>
> **[P] wants** — to give their judgment efficiently and keep things moving.
> - *push* — being asked to approve everything, including what I always approve, makes me the bottleneck.
> - *pull* — if routine calls got out of my way, I'd spend judgment only where it's needed.
> - *anxiety* — if approvals become automatic, I'll rubber-stamp something I should have caught.
> - *habit* — I re-check the agent's work before approving because I don't trust the summary.
>
> **[A] wants** — to get the judgment it needs at the right moment and grain.
> - *anxiety* — ask at the wrong grain and I either pester or overstep.
>
> **Outcome** — The person keeps things moving without becoming the bottleneck or the rubber stamp; the
> agent gets decided-upon at the right cadence and remembers the decision.

## J6 · Steering work in flight
> **Situation** — When an agent is partway through a run and the person realizes it's drifting, or the
> context changed, or they've spotted a boundary it doesn't know about — and it's already done real work
> they don't want to throw away.
>
> **[P] wants** — to reshape what it's doing without starting over.
> - *push* — today my only real lever is to kill it and re-prompt, discarding its progress.
> - *pull* — if I could nudge, redirect, constrain, or take over one step live, I'd intervene sooner and cheaper.
> - *anxiety* — redirect mid-run and it'll half-apply the change, leaving a broken in-between.
> - *habit* — I let a drifting agent finish and fix it myself, because interrupting feels riskier.
>
> **[A] wants** — to absorb a change mid-work and keep going cleanly.
> - *anxiety* — a redirect that conflicts with what I've done will silently break things unless I can flag it.
>
> **Outcome** — The person course-corrects cheaply and keeps the progress that was still good; the agent
> re-plans, yields, or reconciles without losing the thread.

## J7 · Exploring alternatives
> **Situation** — When the person isn't sure which approach is best, and the honest answer is only
> visible once each is actually tried.
>
> **[P] wants** — to have several approaches pursued at once and compared.
> - *push* — picking blind, up front, means committing before I can see the real trade-offs.
> - *pull* — if I could see two real results side by side, I'd choose well instead of guessing.
> - *anxiety* — running several will be wasteful, or the comparison will be apples-to-oranges.
>
> **[A] wants** — to pursue a distinct approach without stepping on the others.
>
> **Outcome** — The person chooses from real results rather than a guess; the divergent work stays
> cleanly separable so the comparison is fair and the losers discardable.

## J8 · Working on the same thing together
> **Situation** — When an agent and the person are making something together and the person is in the
> flow of it — working one part while the agent could be working another — but every tool makes them
> stop, describe, submit, and wait.
>
> **[P] wants** — both of us working on the same thing at once, each on our part.
> - *push* — the stop-describe-submit-wait loop breaks my flow every time.
> - *push* — describing which part I mean, in words, is slower than showing it.
> - *pull* — if it worked alongside me live, filling in around me, it'd feel like real collaboration.
> - *anxiety* — react to my half-finished work and it'll act on something I hadn't decided.
> - *anxiety* — if we're both editing, it'll overwrite the very thing I'm holding.
> - *habit* — I take turns with it because simultaneous feels like it'd collide.
>
> **[A] wants** — to contribute live around what the person is doing.
> - *push* — waiting for a submitted turn wastes the time I could be helping.
> - *anxiety* — touch what they're holding and I've become a hindrance, not a help.
>
> **Outcome** — The person feels they're working *with* the agent, not dispatching to it and waiting; the
> agent helps in parallel, on the right part, attributed, without disturbing what the person holds.

## J9 · Being shown things I didn't ask for
> **Situation** — When an agent, in the course of its work, turns up something the person didn't ask for
> but would want — a finding, a risk, a thing it made — while the person is in the middle of something
> else entirely.
>
> **[P] wants** — it surfaced at the right intensity for its worth and their moment.
> - *push* — the best things agents find are things I didn't know to ask for; I don't want to lose them.
> - *push* — but unrequested stuff dumped at the wrong moment is exactly what makes me mute tools.
> - *anxiety* — let agents surface freely and I'll drown in "look at this" and tune it all out.
> - *habit* — I keep proactive suggestions off because historically they've been noise.
>
> **[A] wants** — to present something valuable that has no place in the person's current view.
> - *anxiety* — present it wrong and I'm the noise; hold it back and I've wasted a genuine find.
>
> **Outcome** — The person benefits from the unasked-for without being interrupted by it; the agent shares
> real value at an intensity matched to its worth and the person's state.

## J10 · Building & adjusting trust
> **Situation** — When an agent has done a run of good work and the person is *still* approving its every
> action out of caution — even as they keep approving them unchanged — while for other things they
> wouldn't dream of letting it act alone.
>
> **[P] wants** — to hand it the right latitude, and change that as it earns or loses it.
> - *push* — supervising everything, including what it's proven on, is tedious and makes me the bottleneck.
> - *pull* — letting it run where it's earned it would free me and speed everything up.
> - *anxiety* — the one time I stop watching is the time it'll do the thing I'd have caught.
> - *anxiety* — grant freedom and will I still see what it did and be able to pull it back?
> - *habit* — I over-supervise because a mistake feels far more expensive than my time.
>
> **[A] wants** — to know and honor its bounds — free where trusted, asking where not.
> - *anxiety* — misjudge my latitude and I either overstep or over-ask.
>
> **Outcome** — The person delegates more as trust grows and pulls back when it breaks, never losing the
> ability to catch a mistake or reclaim control; the agent acts at exactly the latitude it's been given,
> and never crosses a hard limit.

## J11 · Many agents at once
> **Situation** — When the person has several agents on interlocking pieces of a larger thing, and
> orchestrating each one themselves — routing, sequencing, spotting overlaps — is becoming its own
> full-time job.
>
> **[P] wants** — them to work together without being the switchboard.
> - *push* — hand-routing every step just moves the bottleneck to me.
> - *pull* — if they coordinated and handed off among themselves, I could delegate the whole, not the pieces.
> - *anxiety* — coordinate on their own and two of them will quietly conflict or undo each other.
> - *anxiety* — if I'm not routing it, I'll lose the thread of how it all connects.
> - *habit* — I keep everything going through me because that's the only way I see the whole.
>
> **[A] wants** (and second agents) — to coordinate and hand off directly with peers.
> - *anxiety* — a silent conflict with another agent means we undo each other's work.
>
> **Outcome** — The person scales their reach across many agents without becoming their switchboard, and
> still sees how it connects; agents coordinate and surface conflicts rather than colliding.

## J12 · Being known
> **Situation** — When the person works with agents day after day across all their interrelated projects,
> and keeps having to re-establish who they are, how they like things, and what their context is — every
> session and every agent.
>
> **[P] wants** — agents to know them and their whole world.
> - *push* — repeating my preferences and context endlessly is exhausting and I resent it.
> - *push* — the value of my work is in how projects connect; an agent that sees one silo misses the point.
> - *pull* — if they knew me deeply, I'd get help that fits and finally stop repeating myself.
> - *anxiety* — the deeper they know me, the more it matters that my private context stays mine.
> - *anxiety* — if their model of me is wrong and I can't see or fix it, they'll act on a wrong "me."
> - *habit* — I re-brief from scratch because I don't trust that what I said carried over.
>
> **[A] wants** — to remember and apply what it knows, and correct it when wrong.
> - *push* — without memory I'm useless-until-briefed every time.
> - *anxiety* — act on a stale or wrong belief about them and I damage the relationship.
>
> **Outcome** — The person stops repeating themselves and gets help that fits, across their whole life,
> with their context under their control; the agent applies what it knows unprompted and updates when wrong.

## J13 · Wherever & whenever I am
> **Situation** — When the person moves through their day across apps, rooms, and devices — heads-down,
> then in a meeting, then away, then back — while agents keep working, and the only way to supervise is
> to go to a separate place and stop what they're doing.
>
> **[P] wants** — agents present around them and reachable anywhere, behaving according to where they are.
> - *push* — leaving my work to go to a separate place to supervise breaks my day.
> - *push* — agents that reach me one way regardless of whether I'm focused or asleep are useless or intrusive.
> - *pull* — if they lived around my work, knew my state, and could act on my real files and accounts, supervising would cost almost nothing.
> - *anxiety* — for agents to act on my real environment, I have to trust them with real access.
> - *anxiety* — something always-present over everything could become a constant low-grade intrusion.
> - *habit* — I wall agent work off in its own app because letting it near everything feels risky.
>
> **[A] wants** — to sense the person's context and act on their real environment within permission.
> - *push* — blind to their state, I reach them at the wrong time; without real access, I can only advise.
> - *anxiety* — act on their real things wrongly and the damage is real, not sandboxed.
>
> **Outcome** — The person supervises and is reached anywhere, in-context, without going to a separate
> place, and agents get real work done on their real things; the agent reaches them well and acts safely.

## J14 · Work across time
> **Situation** — When work is too big for one sitting — it must run in the background, or overnight, or
> for weeks — and the person has had agents run off unattended and either go far wrong before they
> noticed or produce a pile whose state they can't tell.
>
> **[P] wants** — agents to work over long horizons and present results when it's right.
> - *push* — much of what I want done doesn't fit the time I can watch it.
> - *pull* — if agents worked reliably in the background and overnight, I'd wake to progress.
> - *anxiety* — unattended, an agent goes a long way in the wrong direction before I catch it.
> - *anxiety* — a long run produces a heap I have to reconstruct before I can trust it.
> - *habit* — I keep tasks short and supervised because I don't trust long unattended runs.
>
> **[A] wants** — to work unattended and know when to pause for input.
> - *anxiety* — run too long the wrong way and I waste hours; pause too often and I'm not autonomous.
>
> **Outcome** — The person gets things done that don't fit a single sitting and trusts the results
> waiting for them; the agent sustains progress and checks in at the right moments.

## J15 · Recovering & staying safe
> **Situation** — When agents act in the person's real world — files, accounts, things with consequences
> — and any of them could make a mistake, some of which can't be taken back.
>
> **[P] wants** — to recover from mistakes and be protected from irreversible ones.
> - *push* — the fear of an unrecoverable mistake is what most holds me back from delegating boldly.
> - *pull* — if I knew I could always undo, and irreversible things would stop and check, I'd hand over far more.
> - *anxiety* — an agent could do something destructive faster than I can react.
> - *anxiety* — when something breaks, I won't be able to tell what happened or how to fix it.
> - *habit* — I keep agents away from anything consequential because I can't trust the safety net.
>
> **[A] wants** — to stay reversible, careful before harm, and honest about failure.
> - *anxiety* — an irreversible mistake I make can't be undone, and a hidden failure destroys trust.
>
> **Outcome** — The person delegates boldly, knowing mistakes are recoverable and the irreversible ones
> guarded; the agent acts reversibly, stops before the irreversible, and reports failure truthfully.

## J16 · Working with other people
> **Situation** — When work involves other people — a teammate, a collaborator — each with their own
> agents, and the work and its supervision need to cross between them.
>
> **[P] wants** — their agents to work across people, not just for them alone.
> - *push* — agent work trapped as solo-only can't join the actual teams I work in.
> - *pull* — if my agents and a colleague's could combine, we'd multiply the whole team.
> - *anxiety* — sharing an agent risks leaking my private context into someone else's view.
> - *anxiety* — handing off supervision could drop the thread of what the agent was doing.
> - *habit* — I keep agents personal because sharing feels like it'd blur ownership and privacy.
>
> **[A] wants** — to serve more than one person, respecting each one's authority and privacy.
> - *anxiety* — carry one person's private context into another's view and I've breached trust.
>
> **Outcome** — The person brings agents into a team without leaking what's theirs or dropping the work;
> the agent works across people cleanly, keeping each one's context and authority separate.

## J17 · Finding & growing capability
> **Situation** — When the person wants something done and doesn't know whether it's possible, which
> agent does it, or how to make an agent do a thing it can't yet — and keeps re-teaching the same
> workflows.
>
> **[P] wants** — to know what's possible and grow new capability.
> - *push* — not knowing what my agents can do means I under-use them; re-teaching wastes effort.
> - *pull* — if capability were discoverable and teachable, I'd keep expanding what agents do for me.
> - *anxiety* — teaching an agent might be more work than just doing it myself.
> - *habit* — I stick to the few uses I know work and do the rest by hand.
>
> **[A] wants** — to be discoverable and to learn from being shown.
> - *push* — capability the person can't find or grow is capability wasted.
>
> **Outcome** — The person keeps expanding what agents do for them without re-teaching from scratch; the
> agent's abilities are findable and it improves from what it's shown.

## J18 · Interacting naturally
> **Situation** — When the person engages with an agent in the middle of everything else — sometimes at a
> keyboard, sometimes hands full, sometimes eyes elsewhere, sometimes mid-sentence with another person.
>
> **[P] wants** — to use whatever mode fits the moment, and switch freely.
> - *push* — being forced into one input mode when it doesn't fit makes the tool unusable in the moment.
> - *pull* — if I could speak, type, touch, or point as fits, agents would fit into my real life.
> - *anxiety* — switching modes mid-interaction usually means losing the thread and starting over.
> - *habit* — I default to typing at the desk because it's the only mode I trust to carry context.
>
> **[A] wants** — to receive intent and respond through whatever channel fits the person's situation.
>
> **Outcome** — The person interacts naturally instead of adapting to the tool, switching modes without
> losing the thread; the agent meets them in whatever channel their moment calls for.

## J19 · Cost, limits & accountability
> **Situation** — When agents run on real resources — the person's time, money, compute — and can work
> unattended and at length, so a task can quietly run up more than intended.
>
> **[P] wants** — visibility into cost and the ability to bound it.
> - *push* — unbounded agent work could run up cost I only discover after the fact.
> - *pull* — if I could see and cap the spend, I'd let agents run longer and freer without dread.
> - *anxiety* — a background agent will burn resources on the wrong thing while I'm not looking.
> - *habit* — I keep agent work short and watched partly to keep a lid on cost.
>
> **[A] wants** — to track its own cost and flag before exceeding a limit.
> - *anxiety* — overrun silently and I've betrayed the person's budget and trust.
>
> **Outcome** — The person lets agents run without fear of runaway cost; the agent stays within bounds
> and warns early when a task is bigger than it looked.

## J20 · Finishing, saving & reusing
> **Situation** — When a piece of agent work is done and was useful — or nearly so — and the person will
> likely want it, or something like it, again, but it's about to scroll off into the churn of a busy
> workspace.
>
> **[P] wants** — to keep, revisit, and reuse it.
> - *push* — good work that vanishes into the churn is value lost, and I rebuild it next time.
> - *pull* — if useful work could be kept and made repeatable, value would compound instead of resetting.
> - *anxiety* — saving and organizing everything becomes its own pile to manage.
> - *habit* — I let things scroll away and re-do them, because saving-and-finding is more hassle than redoing.
>
> **[A] wants** — to hand back a usable result and stay inspectable after the session.
>
> **Outcome** — The person compounds value across time instead of starting over; the agent's output is
> keepable, repeatable, and revisitable.

## J21 · Knowing & bounding my exposure
> **Situation** — When the person's agents run on LLMs — their own or a third party's — their data and
> context flow out to those models to get the work done, and the person can't see what's leaving, to
> whom, or whether it's kept.
>
> **[P] wants** — to see what's being sent and to whom, and to bound it.
> - *push* — I can't judge a risk I can't see, so I either over-restrict and lose value, or unknowingly over-expose.
> - *pull* — if I could see exactly what leaves, to which model, and what they do with it, I'd set the right boundary and delegate more.
> - *anxiety* — invisible exposure makes me assume the worst and wall agents off from anything sensitive.
> - *habit* — I keep sensitive work away from agents entirely because I can't tell what would leak.
>
> **[A] wants** — to be transparent about what it needs to send, and to work within the boundary set.
> - *anxiety* — send more than the person expected, even usefully, and I've breached a trust whose shape I couldn't see.
>
> **Outcome** — The person sets an informed privacy posture from real visibility, not a blind toggle, and
> agents expose only what's been agreed. *(How private the instance is, is the operator's setting; this
> job is the visibility that makes that setting informed rather than blind.)*

## Cross-cutting constraints & principles

*These have no specific triggering situation — they hold across **every** job above — so they are not
job stories. They are the qualities any solution must have. Kept distinct so we don't mistake a
constraint for a job.*

- **C1 · Control is inviolable** *(invariant)* — The person can always reclaim control; no delegation,
  rule, or agent can lock them out. (The *act* of reclaiming is jobbed in J6/J10; this is the guarantee.)
- **C2 · Nothing becomes noise** *(quality / anti-goal)* — Attention is the scarcest resource; no
  capability may generate noise. Every surfacing must be earned. (This is the outcome J4 and J9 exist to deliver.)
- **C3 · No repetition** *(quality)* — The person never re-establishes what is already known — to an
  agent or across agents. (The outcome J12 delivers.)
- **C4 · Whole-life scope** *(scope constraint)* — Help spans the person's whole interrelated life, not
  one siloed domain; the value is in the connections, so narrowing to one domain is disallowed.
- **C5 · The agent is a participant** *(principle / stance)* — An agent has its own jobs, not merely
  tasks; this is *why* every job above is multi-party.
- **C6 · Graceful degradation** *(robustness constraint)* — It works under reduced conditions — fewer
  agents, less trust, no network, another device — just smaller.
